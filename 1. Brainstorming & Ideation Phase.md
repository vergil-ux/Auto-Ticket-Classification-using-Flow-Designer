# Brainstorming & Ideation Phase

## Where this idea came from

I picked this project after noticing how repetitive ticket handling is at a typical school IT helpdesk. Every day the same handful of issues show up — Wi-Fi not connecting, a projector refusing to turn on, someone forgetting their password, or a lab computer running painfully slow. Every one of these tickets has to be opened, read, and manually categorized by an IT staff member before it can even be assigned to the right person. That's a lot of repetitive manual work for something that's honestly pretty predictable once you look at the pattern.

That got me thinking — if the *type* of issue is basically obvious from a couple of keywords in the description, why is a human still doing that step by hand every time?

## The problem, in plain terms

The school IT helpdesk receives multiple incident requests daily from students and teachers — Wi-Fi issues, projector failures, password problems, slow computers. Currently, IT staff manually reviews each request and assigns a category, which is time-consuming and inefficient. It works, but it doesn't scale well, and it's an easy place for delays and inconsistency to creep in (two agents might categorize the same complaint differently).

## What I considered

Before settling on an approach I looked at a few options:

- **Just make agents categorize faster / add a checklist** — doesn't actually remove the manual step, just makes it slightly less painful.
- **A full machine-learning classifier** — technically possible with ServiceNow's Predictive Intelligence, but that felt like overkill for a helpdesk that mostly deals with 4–5 very predictable issue types, and it would need a decent amount of training data and ongoing retraining that I didn't have for this project.
- **Keyword-based auto-classification with Flow Designer** — this felt like the sweet spot. It's a no-code approach, easy to explain to anyone maintaining it later, and it directly matches the way these tickets already read (short descriptions like "WiFi not working" or "Projector not turning on" basically classify themselves).

I went with the last option.

## The idea

Use ServiceNow **Flow Designer** to scan the Short Description (and Description) of a new ticket for known keywords — Wi-Fi/Network, Projector, Password/Login, Slow/Hanging — the moment it's created, and automatically fill in the Category and Subcategory without an agent touching either field. Once it's classified, the flow also fires off a confirmation email to whoever raised the ticket so they know it's been logged.

## Why this makes sense for a school IT desk specifically

- Most tickets fall into a small, repeatable set of categories, so a keyword match is genuinely reliable here — this isn't a huge, unpredictable enterprise helpdesk.
- No-code means anyone taking over this system later (even a non-developer) can open the flow and see exactly what it's doing.
- It removes a manual, repetitive step from the IT team's day without needing any AI/ML infrastructure.

## Objectives

- Automatically classify incidents at the time of creation.
- Reduce manual effort for IT agents.
- Improve ticket routing efficiency.
- Implement a no-code, easily maintainable solution.

## What I expect to get out of it

- Tickets get classified instantly instead of sitting in a queue waiting on a human to read and sort them.
- Category/Subcategory values stay consistent because they're not being picked by different people on different days.
- Callers get an automatic acknowledgment email instead of silence after submitting a ticket.
- The whole thing is simple enough to extend later — add a keyword, add a category, done.
